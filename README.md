# Global Unemployment and Economic Trend Analysis
### Impact of the 2008 Financial Crisis and COVID-19 on Total and Youth Unemployment Across 24 Countries (2000–2025)

## 📌 Project Overview
This project analyzes GDP growth, total unemployment, and youth unemployment across 24 countries using World Bank data from 2000 to 2025. It compares developed and developing economies, investigates the impact of major economic crises, and examines the relationship between GDP growth and unemployment using Python and statistical analysis.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Examine changes in GDP growth and unemployment over time.
- Compare economic growth and unemployment between developed and developing countries.
- Compare youth unemployment with total unemployment and examine how it changes during economic crises.
- Compare the effects of the 2008 financial crisis and COVID-19 on economic growth and employment.
- Measure the relationship between GDP growth and unemployment using correlation analysis.

## 🛠️ Tools & Technologies
- **Language:** Python
- **Data Analysis:** Pandas, NumPy
- **Visualization:** Matplotlib, Seaborn
- **Environment:** Jupyter Notebook

## 📊 Key Findings
- GDP growth declined sharply during the 2009 financial crisis period and the 2020 COVID-19 shock.
- Average youth unemployment (18.1%) was substantially higher than total unemployment (7.8%).
- Developing countries recorded higher average GDP growth than developed countries, while average unemployment was nearly equal.
- GDP growth and unemployment showed a moderate negative correlation of **-0.39**.

## ⚠️ Limitations
- The analysis does not include all factors that influence economic growth and unemployment.
- Correlation identifies an association but does not prove cause and effect.
